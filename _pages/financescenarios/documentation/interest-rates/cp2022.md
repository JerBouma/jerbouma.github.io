Dutch pension funds test their plans on the scenario sets De Nederlandsche Bank (DNB) publishes every quarter: 20,000 scenarios over 100 years from the CP2022 model of the [Commissie Parameters (2022)](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/documenten/rapporten/2022/11/29/bijlage-2-technische-appendix){:target="_blank"}. Finance Scenarios runs that model as its Technical Appendix writes it (`method: cp2022`), with the parameters DNB publishes, so a run can rebuild DNB's real-world set or put DNB's economy next to factors of its own.

## The Model

Three state variables move together: a Heston-type stochastic variance $$v$$ that sets how large the shocks are, the nominal short rate $$r$$ and expected euro-area inflation $$\pi$$ (Technical Appendix, eqs. 1-6). They revert to their long-run levels through a 3 x 3 matrix $$K$$ whose first row is zero off the diagonal (eq. 3): the variance reverts on its own, while all three states pull on the rate and on expected inflation. In matrix form:

$$
dX_t = K\,(\mathbb{E}[X_\infty] - X_t)\,dt + \Sigma(v_t)\,dW_t, \qquad X_t = (v_t, r_t, \pi_t)^\top
$$

$$W_t$$ holds five independent shocks, the first scaled by $$\sqrt{v_t}$$ and the others by $$\sqrt{1 + \gamma_i v_t}$$; the three states load on the first three. The log equity index and the log HICP, the euro area's consumer price index, drift at $$r + \eta_S$$ and $$\pi + \eta_\Pi$$ (with $$\eta_S$$ and $$\eta_\Pi$$ their risk premia) less half their variance, and load on all five shocks, so equity falls when the variance rises.

Nominal and real zero-coupon bond prices of every whole maturity $$\tau$$ from 1 to 100 years are exponential-affine in the state, their logarithm linear in it:

$$
P(t, \tau) = \exp\!\big(\phi(t, \tau) + \psi(\tau)^\top X_t\big), \qquad z(t, \tau) = P(t, \tau)^{-1/\tau} - 1
$$

with $$z$$ the annually compounded zero rate. The loadings $$\psi$$ solve the Riccati equations of the appendix (section 3, eqs. 14-19, and section 10, eqs. 76-79), and the package's own solution reproduces DNB's published $$\psi$$ to within $$5 \times 10^{-6}$$. The function $$\phi$$ also carries the time-varying price of risk of section 6 (eqs. 26-40), fitted to curves DNB does not publish, so a run reads both functions from the tables DNB publishes each quarter.

Dutch CPI inflation is euro-area HICP inflation plus a spread chosen so that the scenarios' mean Dutch log inflation follows the forecast path of the CPB (the Netherlands Bureau for Economic Policy Analysis), read from DNB's set, and 2% after that path ends (section 9.1, eqs. 54-55). The run steps the model monthly with the appendix's own scheme (section 9.1, eqs. 51-53), with [Andersen's (2008)](https://doi.org/10.21314/jcf.2008.189){:target="_blank"} quadratic-exponential step for the variance; the appendix adds a martingale correction to that step, which the package leaves out.

## The Parameters

Each quarterly workbook DNB publishes carries the model's parameters on its `0_Parameters` sheet, next to the term-structure functions of the nominal and real curves and the paths of the three state variables. The package stores each quarter as a vintage, and a run reads the one DNB had published by its calibration date; `python -m financescenarios.anchors update` checks DNB's page and stores a new quarter. The 2026Q4 set ships with the package.

The parameters can also be backed out of a set's own 20,000 paths. `estimate_cp2022` matches the exact yearly moments of the monthly scheme to two sets of regressions on the paths, 48 moments in all: the yearly moves regressed on the starting state, and the products of their residuals regressed on the starting variance. It does so by minimum distance (the parameters whose moments come closest to the paths'), with the Commissie's constraints and the 5.4% gross equity return imposed, and takes its standard errors from 50 bootstrap draws. On the 2026Q4 set every well-identified parameter comes back within about two standard errors of DNB's (with $$K_{\pi r}$$ DNB's label for the pull of expected inflation's gap on the rate):

| Parameter (DNB's label) | DNB | Estimated (standard error) |
|:--|--:|--:|
| $$K_{vv}$$ | 2.7889 | 2.7892 (0.018) |
| $$K_{rr}$$ | 0.4827 | 0.4847 (0.0011) |
| $$K_{\pi r}$$ | -0.9213 | -0.9242 (0.0027) |
| $$K_{\pi\pi}$$ | 0.5955 | 0.5949 (0.0017) |
| $$\mathbb{E}[v_\infty]$$ | 0.05407 | 0.05408 (0.00005) |
| Equity volatility at $$\mathbb{E}[v]$$ | 15.53% | 15.53% (0.01) |

A variance reverting at a speed of 2.79 a year halves its distance to the long-run level in about three months.

## In a Run

Rates at any whole maturity from 1 to 100 years, on the nominal or the euro-area real curve, euro-area (HICP) or Dutch (CPI) inflation, and one equity can all take `method: cp2022`, and every such entry is an output of the same model. The model's equity, HICP and shortest rate carry the run's correlated draws, and the model turns those into its five independent shocks, so the model's own correlations hold exactly while any other factor in the set correlates with it through the usual estimate on history. The `dnb` factor set holds the euro 1-, 5-, 10-, 20- and 30-year nominal rates, the real 10-year rate, euro-area and Dutch inflation and world equity, plus German unemployment, which is not part of CP2022 but which every run needs. The `dnb` settings run it monthly over 100 years with 2,000 scenarios.

A rate entry of your own takes the same method:

```yaml
# my-project/factor-sets/my_factors.yaml (a factor set)
interest_rates:
  - name: euro_10y
    method: cp2022
    curve: nominal
    maturity: 10
    currency: EUR
    source: oecd # the history its correlation with other factors is estimated on
    country: Germany
    term: long
    period: monthly
```

## Example

The `dnb` settings and factor set run DNB's model as published. To simulate them, 2,000 scenarios over 100 years, and set the run's moments beside those of DNB's 2026Q4 set of 20,000:

```python
from financetoolkit import Toolkit

from financescenarios import Scenarios
from financescenarios.validation.dnb.dnb_controller import Dnb

result = Scenarios.from_profiles(settings="dnb", factor_set="dnb").simulate()

dnb = Dnb(Toolkit(["SPY"], benchmark_ticker=None))
dnb.compare_moments(
    result,
    {
        "Nominal rate 1Y": "euro_1y",
        "Nominal rate 10Y": "euro_10y",
        "Real rate EU 10Y": "euro_real_10y",
        "Inflation EU": "euro_inflation",
        "Inflation NL": "dutch_inflation",
        "Equity return": "world_equity",
    },
)
```

Which returns, for each variable and year, the run's moments next to DNB's. A selection, in percent, run / DNB:

<div class="fs-callout fs-callout--limits" markdown="1">
<p class="fs-callout__title">Limits</p>

- Real world only. DNB's market-consistent set is calibrated to equity options, swaptions and inflation caps and floors (eq. 25), which needs paid market data. DNB's own paths of either set can still be read in with `external_scenario_source` and `provider: dnb`.
- DNB's sets carry a long-run equity return of ln(1.054), the Commissie's advised 5.4% before costs, where eq. 41 states 5.2% after them; DNB's page says to take the 20 basis points of costs off for net returns.
- The term-structure functions carry the fit to DNB's valuation-date curves, whose monthly inputs are not published. Between whole years they are interpolated linearly, so rates are exact at every whole year and close in between.
- The model's scheme is monthly, so a run with `cp2022` entries must step monthly; the configuration refuses another step.
</div>
